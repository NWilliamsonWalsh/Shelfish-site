01

About this policy

Shelf-ish stores and processes the content you choose to capture so you can keep, find and reuse it. We do not operate a server that receives your clips, sell your content, build advertising profiles or use third-party analytics or telemetry in the app or browser extension. This does not mean the software handles no data: it handles your clips and associated information on your devices and, if enabled, through your own iCloud account.

02

Content and purpose

Clips can contain text, links, files, images, screenshots, audio, transcripts, code, locations, contact details, tags, summaries and source metadata. Shelf-ish uses these to provide capture, search, previews, organisation, copying, sharing, expiry and optional on-device intelligence and personal-device sync.

03

Shelf-ish Context for Chrome

The optional browser extension requires a compatible Shelf-ish app on the same Mac. Before capture starts, you must accept its capture disclosure, grant website access and connect the extension to the Mac app. You may enable website access across ordinary websites, pause individual sites or pause capture everywhere.

When you copy selected webpage text, or use the selection context-menu action, the extension reads the selection, page address, title and available source details. Depending on the page, these may include an author or sender, message or comment identifier, message time, heading or code line. It does not invent missing attribution. If you enable surrounding text, it also reads a short nearby passage or a preceding loaded AI prompt associated with the selected response. This can include personal communications or other sensitive information present there.

The extension sends that context to Shelf-ish on your Mac over an authenticated connection restricted to 127.0.0.1. It does not send it to a Shelf-ish server, advertising service or external AI provider. The Mac app can attach it to the matching copied clip; the explicit selection action can import the selection. The extension does not modify your clipboard or request unrestricted clipboard read/write access. It does not record a browsing history or capture every page.

The selected text is limited to 32,000 UTF-8 bytes, surrounding text to 6,000 bytes and the complete capture envelope to 64,000 bytes. Copy snapshots expire after five seconds for transport validation. These are size/transport bounds, not a promise that all personal or sensitive information is detected.

Private browser windows, browser-internal pages, input/password fields, known password-manager and sign-in surfaces, and explicitly private or hidden page elements are excluded. These checks cannot recognise every sensitive webpage or piece of text. Pause a site or disable surrounding text before copying material you do not want included.

04

Shelf-ish Context for Safari

The Safari extension is included in Shelf-ish for Mac. Before it sends context, you accept a one-time capture disclosure in the extension and choose whether to include surrounding text. Safari stores that choice per browser profile. Older Safari installs also need this disclosure; a previous Chrome-style permission or consent flag does not replace it.

The extension requests access to ordinary HTTP and HTTPS websites during setup. Safari controls the website grant, including the option to allow all websites. Shelf-ish does not silently grant access or require an extra enable step for each site. You may deny or restrict access in Safari, pause individual sites, turn off extension capture, or switch off Safari in Shelf-ish Settings → Connectors. Existing global and site pauses are preserved when you accept the disclosure.

Only a trusted Copy event or your explicit selection-capture action sends context. That context can include the selected text, page address/title and available source details. If you choose surrounding text, it can include a nearby passage or preceding loaded AI prompt and may contain personal communications or other sensitive information. The same ordinary-page, sensitive-field and private-window exclusions described above apply. The browser does not request general clipboard read/write access or keep a browsing-history archive.

Safari uses Apple's packaged native messaging and the local Shelf-ish App Group, not Chrome's localhost connection. No pairing code or browser credential is required. The native handler only accepts fresh, bounded envelopes while the Mac host is available and Safari capture is allowed. Captured text is limited to 32,000 UTF-8 bytes, surrounding text to 6,000 bytes and the full envelope to 64,000 bytes. Safari's native freshness limit is three seconds. Transport expiry is not a guarantee of physical deletion while the apps are closed: temporary staging files are consumed and removed by the host or pruned on subsequent writes. Saved clips and context follow the retention, iCloud and deletion rules below.

Safari keeps the disclosure version, surrounding-text choice, capture preference, paused-site list and content-free delivery status in local extension storage. The native App Group also holds a content-free host-availability timestamp and capture-status flags. These are used for the connection status and privacy gates, not analytics. Safari context is not sent to a Shelf-ish server, advertising service or external AI provider. Summaries run on the Mac; saved context may sync through your own iCloud account when sync is active.

Disabling or removing the extension stops future handoffs but does not delete saved clips. Remove source context or delete clips in Shelf-ish to remove saved content; supported deletions propagate when sync is active. Safari manages the extension's website access, profiles and removal of its local storage.

05

Excel clipboard context

When Excel context and automatic clipboard capture are enabled on your Mac, an ordinary copy can keep the selected cells together with the workbook name, worksheet and cell range supplied by Excel. A source link may contain a local file path or a workbook web address when Excel provides one. No Excel add-in or separate account connection is needed for this native clipboard route. Missing or invalid source details are not guessed.

Excel may include an embedded workbook snapshot in the clipboard. Shelf-ish reads that snapshot in memory to extract the copied rows across their table’s columns and available headers. This can include cell values you did not select. For ordinary worksheet ranges, inferred first-row labels are marked as possible headers. Hidden rows and columns are omitted from this extra context. Values can include cached formula results and may differ from Excel’s displayed formatting; Shelf-ish does not execute formulas or follow external workbook links.

The context parser accepts embedded snapshots up to 8 MiB and retains an excerpt of at most 6,000 UTF-8 bytes, with up to 20 selected rows and 64 context columns. The snapshot itself is not retained or uploaded by the connector, and no workbook is opened from disk for enrichment. Unsupported, oversized or unavailable snapshots fall back to context from the copied cells. The original copied selection and its clipboard formats are not changed.

Only the resulting source metadata and bounded excerpt are attached to the saved clip. They follow the storage, search, retention, deletion and optional iCloud sync rules below. Switch off Excel in Settings → Connectors to stop adding this context to future copies. This does not remove context already saved.

06

Shelf-ish Context for Obsidian

The optional Obsidian plug-in runs in Obsidian on your Mac. Enable its connector in Shelf-ish and approve the connection before it can send context. With Context on copy enabled, copying a single selected passage in the active Markdown editor can send the selection, note title, vault name, note path, an Obsidian link and the selected line range. Context is taken from the selected lines and up to three neighbouring lines on each side; the retained excerpt is truncated to at most 6,000 UTF-8 bytes. It uses the current editor text, which can include changes not yet saved to disk.

The plug-in reads that active selection and bounded passage; it does not scan the rest of your vault. Empty or multiple selections, oversized selections, other input fields and recognised credential/key filenames are excluded from context capture. These checks cannot detect every secret or sensitive passage inside an ordinary note. The current plug-in includes nearby text whenever it sends context; it has no separate switch for excluding only that nearby text.

Context is sent to Shelf-ish on the same Mac through an authenticated connection restricted to 127.0.0.1, without following redirects. The plug-in adds no clipboard polling, telemetry, external AI request or persistent capture queue. Selections are limited to 24,000 UTF-8 bytes and complete requests to 64,000 bytes. Connection setup does not replay an earlier copy after approval. Ordinary Copy retains Obsidian’s clipboard behaviour; the separately chosen Copy selection with context command makes a plain-text copy.

The connection credential is held in Obsidian’s SecretStorage, separately from the plug-in’s ordinary settings. This storage is local and vault-scoped, but other installed plug-ins can access its named secrets: it is not an isolated security boundary for Shelf-ish. Normal plug-in settings retain the local connection preference and Context on copy choice, not a history of captured note text. Shelf-ish keeps its side of the approved connection in this Mac’s Keychain; credentials are not included in saved clips or Shelf-ish’s iCloud sync.

Saved note details and excerpts follow the storage and sync rules below. The note link identifies a vault and note, not an exact historical revision or a grant of file access. Opening it elsewhere requires the corresponding vault and note to exist there. Turn off Context on copy to stop ordinary-copy context; the explicit capture command remains separate. Disable the connector or disconnect to stop future handoffs. Disconnect revokes the connection before forgetting its local credential; if revocation fails, the plug-in retains the credential so you can retry. Existing saved context must be removed in Shelf-ish.

07

Shelf-ish Context for VS Code

The optional VS Code extension runs in Visual Studio Code on your Mac. Enable its connector in Shelf-ish and approve the connection before it can send context. With Context on copy enabled, copying a single selection in a named local file editor can send the selected text, filename, workspace-folder name when available, document address, full local file path, selected line range, starting column and language identifier. Context is taken from the selected lines and up to three neighbouring lines on each side; the retained excerpt is truncated to at most 6,000 UTF-8 bytes. It uses the current editor buffer, including edits not yet saved to disk.

The extension does not scan your project or repository. Untitled, remote and virtual documents, empty or multiple selections, oversized selections and recognised credential/key filenames are excluded from context capture. Terminal, chat and other input views retain their existing copy behaviour. Filename checks cannot detect every secret in an ordinary source file. The current extension includes nearby code whenever it sends context; it has no separate switch for excluding only that nearby code.

Context is sent to Shelf-ish on the same Mac through an authenticated connection restricted to 127.0.0.1, without following redirects. The extension adds no clipboard polling, telemetry, external AI request or persistent capture queue. Selections are limited to 24,000 UTF-8 bytes and complete requests to 64,000 bytes. Connection setup does not replay an earlier copy after approval. Ordinary Copy uses VS Code’s native copy command and preserves its plain and rich clipboard formats. The separately chosen Copy Selection with Context command makes a plain-text copy.

The connection credential is stored in VS Code’s SecretStorage, not project settings. VS Code documents this as encrypted storage that does not sync across machines. Shelf-ish keeps its side of the approved connection in this Mac’s Keychain. These credentials are separate from saved context and are not included in clips or Shelf-ish’s iCloud sync. The extension does not keep a separate archive of captured code.

Saved file details and excerpts follow the storage and sync rules below. The source link opens a local file, not an exact saved revision, and may not work on another device or after a file is moved. Turn off Context on copy to stop ordinary-copy context; the explicit capture command remains separate. Disable the connector or disconnect to stop future handoffs. Disconnect revokes the connection before removing its local credential. Existing saved context must be removed in Shelf-ish.

08

Extension settings and connection information

Chrome stores your consent version, capture preferences, paused/previously enabled sites, browser choice, connection/profile identifiers and pairing credential in local extension storage. The credential is restricted to trusted extension contexts. A temporary connection proof is held in trusted session storage or memory and expires after two minutes. Native connection credentials are held in the Mac Keychain. The last delivery status contains a time, success/state and an error code, not clip text, source URLs, filenames or author details. The extension does not keep a separate archive of captured passages.

Disconnect revokes a current per-browser connection. Legacy shared-code clients may only forget their local credential; revoke/rotate that shared code in the Mac app if you need to invalidate all clients using it. Disconnecting or uninstalling the extension does not delete clips already saved in Shelf-ish. Chrome controls removal of its extension storage on uninstall; the Mac connection can also be revoked in Shelf-ish. Paused-site preferences remain until you change them or remove the extension's stored data.

09

Storage, iCloud, retention and deletion

Saved clips, source context and summaries live in Shelf-ish's local app store. When iCloud is enabled and available, clip contents and associated source details may sync through Apple's CloudKit service to your other devices. Apple handles that data under its privacy policy. A local connector handoff does not mean a saved clip will remain on one device. We do not claim that every sync field or every older app version has end-to-end encryption. Concealing a clip is a display/privacy control, not a guarantee that it is excluded from sync.

Saved content follows the app's retention/expiry and pinning settings. You can remove source context or delete a clip in Shelf-ish. With sync active, supported deletions propagate to your devices; backups and Apple's service retention follow their own lifecycle. Disconnecting capture stops new handoffs, not prior copies, exports, backups or saved clips. Local deletion markers may remain to prevent older devices restoring deleted clips; they do not retain the deleted clip body.

These rules also apply to Excel, Obsidian and VS Code context. The saved attachment can contain source titles and links, workbook or vault names, workspace-folder names, local file paths, cell or line ranges, capture time, source identifiers, a digest used to match the copy, a bounded excerpt and an optional on-device summary. Those details are stored with the clip in Shelf-ish’s local app database and can be used in previews and local search. An excerpt can repeat some or all of your selection; it is not an anonymous record.

When sync is active, that saved metadata, excerpt and summary can travel with the clip through your own private iCloud database, including identifying paths, note/vault names and values or text beyond the original selection. A handoff that stays on the same Mac does not make the resulting saved context local-only. Connection credentials and the embedded Excel workbook snapshot are not included in Shelf-ish’s clip sync. Sync carries the saved clip and its context; it does not grant access to, or separately synchronise, the source workbook, vault or project.

Switching a connector off, turning off Context on copy or disconnecting stops the corresponding future capture route; it does not erase prior clips or context. Use Remove source context on the saved source attachment, or delete the clip, to remove saved material. With sync active, supported removals propagate when devices sync. Pinning and expiry apply to these attachments with their clip; copies already exported or shared and service backups have their own retention lifecycle.

10

On-device intelligence and other services

Summaries and other intelligence features use Apple frameworks on your device. The Chrome extension does not itself generate summaries. Neither it nor the Mac context summariser sends your passages to an external AI service. Availability depends on your Mac, OS, language, Apple Intelligence settings and model readiness. Model output can be inaccurate; the captured source remains available to inspect.

Link previews may fetch metadata/images directly from websites you save; those sites receive the network request under their own policies. Apple model assets may be downloaded when needed. Saving to Reminders uses Apple's Reminders store, which can separately sync through iCloud. If you deliberately share/export a clip or contact support, your chosen recipient receives what you send. We do not receive routine clip content or source passages from normal extension use.

11

Permissions and controls

The Chrome extension uses local storage for settings/connection state, scripting and optional website access to read context on authorised pages, active-tab access to identify the current site when you open the menu, and a context menu for explicit selection capture. Its mandatory host access is limited to the local Mac connection. Website permission alone does not replace capture consent. You can manage permissions in Chrome and pause capture in the extension.

The native app requests protected access only for relevant features, such as microphone, speech recognition, system-audio recording, Reminders or input control. You can manage that access in System Settings.

Excel context is controlled in Shelf-ish → Settings → Connectors and requires automatic clipboard capture for ordinary copies. Obsidian and VS Code also require an approved local connection and expose a Context on copy preference in their plug-in or extension. That preference controls ordinary-copy context, while their explicitly chosen capture commands remain separate. To stop all new context from either editor, disable its connector or disconnect it. Nearby note text, code and additional spreadsheet cells may contain sensitive information even if the originally selected text does not.

12

Chrome Web Store Limited Use

Shelf-ish Context's use of information obtained through Chrome APIs complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. We use that information only to provide the disclosed source-context features and their necessary connection/security functions. We do not sell it, use it for advertising or credit/lending decisions, or allow people to read your captured content through a developer-operated service. Any content you expressly send to support is used to handle that request.

13

Children, changes and contact

Shelf-ish is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly receive their personal information through a developer-operated collection service. If data practices materially change, we will update this policy and provide appropriate notice and consent before the changed handling begins.
